MEA reacts as Russia sanctions bill threatening 100% tariff clears US House

H
HT News Desk · New Delhi · Sep 17, 2026 · 4 min read
byline · size 13px · l-height normal · weight 400 · l-spacing normal · Inter

The US House of Representatives passed a sweeping Russia sanctions package on Wednesday, clearing the way for President Donald Trump to impose tariffs of up to 100% on countries that continue buying Russian oil and gas, including India and China. The bill was cleared by a 262-159 vote and now heads to the President's desk, after the Senate approved it last month.

b2 · size 15px · l-height 23.25px (1.55) · weight 400 · l-spacing normal · Noto Sans

What the bill does

h2 · size 22px · l-height 30.8px (1.4) · weight 700 · l-spacing normal · Switzer

The legislation targets Russia's leadership, its energy sector, and vessels accused of helping Moscow evade existing sanctions. It also hands the White House discretionary power to impose steep tariffs on major buyers of Russian crude and natural gas — a provision aimed at reducing India and China's reliance on Russian energy. The tariff isn't automatic: the bill grants the authority, but whether and how it gets used is now up to the President.

b1 · size 17px · l-height 28.05px (1.65) · weight 400 · l-spacing normal · Noto Sans

Debate in the House split largely along party lines, with some lawmakers warning that the bill hands the executive branch unusually broad control over trade policy, even as most members backed the wider push to squeeze Russia's ability to fund the war.

b1 · size 16px · l-height 26.4px (1.65) · weight 400 · l-spacing normal · Noto Sans

India's response

h2 · size 20px · l-height 28px (1.4) · weight 700 · l-spacing normal · Switzer

India's Ministry of External Affairs said it was tracking the legislation closely and had already raised the matter with US counterparts at senior levels. Officials reiterated that energy security for a population of over 1.4 billion depends on a diversified mix of suppliers rather than any single source, and that India would take the steps necessary to protect its trade and economic interests.

b1 · size 16px · l-height 26.4px (1.65) · weight 400 · l-spacing normal · Noto Sans

The message from New Delhi has stayed consistent: energy decisions will be driven by India's own economic interests, not by outside pressure.

pull-quote · size 18px · l-height 27.9px (1.55) · weight 700 italic · l-spacing normal · Switzer

The vote lands just days after Russian President Vladimir Putin and Prime Minister Narendra Modi were seen together at the BRICS summit in New Delhi, a moment that added to scrutiny in Washington over India's ties with Moscow. External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar has separately argued that ending the war in Ukraine will come through dialogue and negotiation, not by cutting off energy trade.
